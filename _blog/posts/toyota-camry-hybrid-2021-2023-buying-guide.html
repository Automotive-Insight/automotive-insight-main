How the 2021 Camry differs from the 2020

This was a mid-life update, so the bones of the car stayed the same. Toyota reshaped the front bumper and grille and redesigned the dashboard. The base Ascent kept a 7-inch screen. Every grade above it got a 9-inch touchscreen with built-in navigation.

Safety took a step forward, with speed sign recognition, emergency steering assist and a system that watches for oncoming traffic when you turn at intersections. The V6 disappeared, and the hybrid was offered in Ascent, Ascent Sport, SX and SL form. According to Drive, more than seven in ten Camry buyers went hybrid. Under the bonnet, it's still a 2.5-litre petrol engine working with electric motors, for 160 kW combined.

What to look at on a used one

Start with the service book. The Camry is due every 12 months or 15,000 km, and a car with gaps in its history deserves a few more questions. Pay attention to where it was serviced, too. Toyota's hybrid battery warranty runs for five years, and it only extends to as much as 10 years if the car has had yearly battery checks and logbook servicing at a Toyota dealer. The seller should be able to tell you, and Toyota can confirm what's still covered on that VIN.

Then the battery itself. The earliest of these cars are now around five years old, so the standard battery cover is running out on some of them. A battery test shows how much capacity is left, which the dashboard won't tell you. While you're in the back seat, look at the battery cooling vent. A vent packed with dust suggests the battery has been running warm.

On the test drive, listen for grinding when you brake and feel for a pulsing pedal. Hybrid brakes don't get used much, and the discs can rust as a result.

Plenty of these Camrys have worked as rideshare or fleet cars. That's not a reason to walk away. High kilometres on a well-serviced hybrid are fine. Just make sure the wear on the seats, steering wheel and suspension matches what the odometer says.

Known issues and recalls

The 2021 to 2023 Camry Hybrid has a good reputation for reliability, and there's no widely reported pattern of hybrid system trouble on this model.

There is one recall to know about. Toyota recall REC-006390 covers almost 70,000 vehicles built from 2022 to 2025, including some Camry Hybrids, for an instrument cluster that can stay blank when the car starts. It mostly affects later builds. Run the VIN through vehiclerecalls.gov.au or ask a Toyota dealer to check.

Running costs and servicing

Toyota claimed 4.2 L/100km for the Ascent and Ascent Sport hybrids, 4.5 for the SL and 4.7 for the SX. Capped-price servicing was $200 a visit when these cars were new, rising to $250 from January 2023.

What changed on the 2021 Camry Hybrid?

A new front end, a redesigned dashboard, a bigger screen on most grades and extra safety tech.

Is a 2021 to 2023 Camry Hybrid a good used buy?

Usually, yes. Just make sure the service history is solid and the battery tests well.

How often does it need servicing?

Every 12 months or 15,000 km, whichever you hit first.

Will servicing it outside Toyota affect the warranty?

The standard warranty stays in place. The battery cover beyond five years depends on servicing and health checks at Toyota.

How much fuel does it use?

Toyota's official figures range from 4.2 to 4.7 L/100km, depending on the grade.
