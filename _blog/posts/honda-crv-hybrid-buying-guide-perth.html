Plenty of the overseas reviews you'll find for the CR-V Hybrid don't really apply in Australia. Honda sold hybrid CR-Vs in other countries for years, but it didn't bring one here until October 2023. That means every locally delivered car is still fairly new, and it changes what you should be looking for when you buy one.

Key takeaways

  • Honda Australia started selling the CR-V Hybrid in October 2023, and all-wheel-drive versions followed in March 2026.
  • Before you buy, check the battery, the service history and how the car has been used.
  • It's due for a service every 12 months or 10,000 km.
  • It costs less to run than the petrol CR-V, particularly around town.
  • It isn't built for towing.

Which CR-V Hybrids were sold here

From late 2023 until 2025, there was just one hybrid on offer: the e:HEV RS. It's front-wheel drive, with a 2.0-litre petrol engine and two electric motors producing 135 kW and 335 Nm. It launched at $59,900 drive-away.

In March 2026 Honda widened the range to four hybrids. The e:HEV X and L are front-wheel drive, and the LX and RS now come with all-wheel drive.

So if you're shopping for a used one in Perth right now, it'll almost certainly be a front-wheel-drive RS. If a seller describes an older CR-V as a hybrid, check the badge and the compliance plate. Honda Australia simply didn't sell one before late 2023.

It doesn't drive quite like a Toyota

Honda's hybrid system works differently from Toyota's. Most of the time an electric motor turns the wheels, while the petrol engine acts as a generator. On the highway, the engine can take over and drive the wheels directly. The battery is small, at just over one kilowatt-hour, and it's charging and discharging almost constantly.

You'll notice this on a test drive. The engine sometimes revs up or settles down without any change in your speed. That's completely normal and nothing to worry about.

What to check before you buy

Look at the service book first. The CR-V Hybrid is due every 12 months or 10,000 km, which is a shorter distance than many owners realise. A two-year-old car that's done 30,000 km should already have three services recorded.

Next, find out how the car has been used. Honda's warranty runs for five years with unlimited kilometres, and the hybrid battery has its own eight-year cover. Cars used for rideshare, deliveries or other commercial work are treated differently, though, with the warranty capped at five years or 140,000 km. It's a fair question to ask the seller.

The battery deserves a proper test, which tells you far more than a spin around the block. Because this battery works so hard, we also make sure nothing's blocking its cooling airflow.

Finish with the brakes, tyres and suspension. Discs on cars that don't get driven much can rust, and uneven tyre wear or kerb damage is easy to spot if you look. If you're buying one of the newer all-wheel-drive models, it'll most likely still be under warranty, but listen for any noise or vibration from the driveline as you pull away.

Common issues

The CR-V Hybrid hasn't been on sale here long, and there's no widely reported pattern of hybrid faults on local cars yet. That makes the basics more important. Check for warning lights and stored fault codes, and confirm that any open recall work has been done. You can run the VIN through vehiclerecalls.gov.au, and a Honda dealer can confirm too.

Running costs and servicing

Honda's 2026 figures put the hybrid at 5.5 to 5.7 L/100km, compared with 7.1 to 7.3 for the petrol. Real-world tests line up well. Drive recorded 5.7 L/100km, and CarExpert's long-term RS averaged 5.6. Over 15,000 km a year, you'd save roughly 240 litres, and the hybrid is happy on regular 91.

The purchase price is where it loses ground. When new, the entry hybrid cost $5,000 more than the entry petrol, so fuel savings take a while to make up the difference. Towing is limited, too, with the e:HEV RS rated to just 750 kg braked.
