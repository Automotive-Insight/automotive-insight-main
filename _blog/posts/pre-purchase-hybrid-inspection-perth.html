A used hybrid can look spotless and drive beautifully on a ten-minute test drive while its battery is well past its best. Nothing on the dashboard will warn you. That's what makes buying one a bit different, because the most expensive parts of the car are the ones you can't see, hear or feel, and a general pre-purchase check usually doesn't test them.

Key takeaways

  • A general pre-purchase inspection often skips the hybrid system entirely.
  • The battery test is the single most important check on a used hybrid.
  • Hybrid brakes can hide rust and sticking calipers behind healthy-looking pads.
  • Plug-in hybrids need their charging system checked too.
  • Ask where the car was serviced, because some battery warranties depend on it.

What a general inspection can miss

A standard pre-purchase check covers the engine, body, suspension, tyres and brakes, and a hybrid needs all of that. What it doesn't usually include is the high-voltage battery, the inverter and its cooling, the electric motors and their wiring, or a scan of the hybrid system for stored faults. Those are the parts that cost the most to fix, and a hybrid can drive perfectly normally while one of them is quietly failing.

Why the battery test comes first

No warning light doesn't mean a healthy battery. We run a capacity and cell-balance test, which shows how much energy the battery can still hold and whether any cells are falling behind the rest. We also check how the battery has been cooled. On many Toyota hybrids that means looking at a small vent near the back seat. If it's clogged, the battery has probably been running hot.

The battery also ties into the warranty. Toyota covers its hybrid batteries for five years, and extends that to as much as 10 years only for cars with yearly health checks and servicing at Toyota. Mitsubishi's extended cover also depends on dealer servicing. So ask the seller where the car has been serviced, and check with the manufacturer what cover is still active on that VIN.

Brakes that look better than they are

With the electric motor doing so much of the slowing, hybrid brakes spend a lot of time doing nothing. The pads can look nearly new while the discs have rusted and the calipers have started to stick. We check pads, discs, calipers and brake fluid, and make sure the regenerative braking is working properly.

Looking into the hybrid system

We scan every control module for current and stored faults, including any that haven't triggered a warning light. On all-wheel-drive hybrids with an electric rear motor, like the RAV4 Hybrid, we inspect the motor and its high-voltage connectors for corrosion. We also look for signs of past accident repairs near high-voltage parts.

Plug-in hybrids

If you're buying a plug-in hybrid, the charging system needs checking as well. We inspect the charge port and pins, look over the cable, make sure the car charges and stops properly, and scan for charging faults. It's also worth comparing the car's current electric range with what it could do when new, because a big gap points to battery wear. Why does a used hybrid need a different inspection?

A general check usually doesn't test the high-voltage battery or hybrid system, and they're the most expensive parts to fix.

How do you test a hybrid battery?

With diagnostic equipment that measures capacity and cell balance. A test drive can't tell you this.

What else gets checked?

The brakes and calipers, stored hybrid faults, the cooling systems and, on AWD models, the rear electric motor and its connectors.

Do you inspect plug-in hybrids?

Yes. For plug-ins, we check the charging port, the cable and the charging system as well.
