EV servicing

Electric vehicle servicing in Perth: what EV owners actually need

What an EV service covers once the oil changes are gone, and the two things that wear faster than owners expect.

4 August 20264 min readAutomotive Insight, Shenton Park

0482 097 182

No oil to change, no spark plugs, no exhaust. It's easy to see why some EV owners wonder whether they need a service at all. They do, but it's a shorter list than you're used to, and a few items on it are new. This is what an electric car really needs, what it can skip, and what to watch as it gets older in Perth's heat.

Key takeaways

  • EVs still need tyre rotations, brake checks, brake fluid, cabin filters, coolant and a healthy 12-volt battery.
  • Oil changes, spark plugs, timing belts and exhaust repairs are gone.
  • Each brand sets its own schedule, and Tesla doesn't use a fixed annual service.
  • EV batteries lose capacity slowly, but heat and lots of fast charging speed it up.
  • Servicing with an independent workshop won't void your standard warranty.

What still needs doing

Tyres come first. EVs are heavy and deliver their power instantly, and Michelin says they can wear tyres up to 20 per cent faster than a petrol car. Tesla recommends rotating them every 10,000 km.

Brakes are the next. Regenerative braking means the pads last a long time, but the discs can rust and the calipers can stick from lack of use. The brake fluid still ages too. Tesla suggests testing it every four years, and other brands, including Kia on the EV6, replace it at set intervals.

Then there's the cabin air filter, which Tesla recommends changing every two years on the Model 3 and Model Y. Most other brands swap it at every service. The battery and motors have their own coolant, changed on a long interval set by the manufacturer. Some EVs, the Kia EV6 among them, also have gear oil that gets checked or changed on schedule.

And don't forget the small 12-volt battery. It runs the car's electronics, and if it goes flat, your EV won't start, however much charge the main battery has.

What you can forget about

There's no engine oil or oil filter to change, and no spark plugs, timing belt, fuel filter, exhaust or clutch. That's a big part of why EVs usually cost less to service over the years, and why some brands have stretched their intervals. Hyundai, for example, moved the Ioniq 5 and Ioniq 6 to a service every 24 months or 30,000 km.

Tesla and everyone else

Tesla says its cars don't need traditional annual maintenance. Instead, it lists individual jobs by time or distance, like cabin filters, tyre rotations and brake fluid tests. Most other brands stick with a regular schedule. BYD's Atto 3 is serviced every 12 months or 20,000 km, and Hyundai's Ioniq models every 24 months or 30,000 km. Your logbook or the manufacturer's app will tell you what applies to your car.

Since July 2022, car makers have had to share service and repair information with independent workshops, including for EVs. The most sensitive high-voltage safety information comes with extra checks on who can access it.

Keeping an eye on the battery

EV batteries fade slowly. A study of more than 22,700 EVs, published in 2026 by fleet data company Geotab, found average capacity loss of 2.3 per cent a year. Cars that mostly used fast chargers above 100 kW lost up to 3 per cent a year, while those that mostly charged on AC lost about 1.5 per cent. Hot climates added around 0.4 per cent a year, which is worth keeping in mind in Perth.

A sudden drop in range, slower charging than usual or a battery warning are all reasons to get it checked. A battery health test also gives you a baseline to compare against in future.

Charging problems

When home charging becomes unreliable, the problem could be the car, the cable or the wall unit, and a proper diagnosis tells you which. At a service, we check the charge port and pins for heat damage or corrosion, look over your charging cable, confirm AC charging works as it should and scan for stored charging faults. You're welcome to use our onsite charger while your car is with us, subject to availability when you book.

Independent workshop or dealer?

Your consumer guarantees under Australian Consumer Law apply whether or not an independent workshop services your car. Manufacturer warranties typically let you choose where you service, as long as the work meets their standards. Recall work and some warranty repairs still have to go through the dealer. For everyday servicing, tyres, brakes, diagnostics and charging faults, an independent EV workshop makes good sense.

We service EVs from Tesla, Hyundai, Kia, BYD, Polestar and Volvo. If there's a warning light on, EV diagnostics is the place to start.

Common questions

Do electric cars need servicing?

Yes, just less of it. Tyres, brakes, brake fluid, filters, coolant and the 12-volt battery all still need attention.

How often should my EV be serviced?

It depends on the brand. Some use a 12 or 24-month schedule, while Tesla lists jobs by time and distance instead.

Will servicing my EV outside the dealer void the warranty?

Not under Australian Consumer Law, provided the work meets the manufacturer's standards.

How quickly do EV batteries lose capacity?

About 2.3 per cent a year on average in one large study. Heat and frequent fast charging make it faster.

Do you service Teslas?

Yes. We handle tyres, brakes, brake fluid, filters, diagnostics and more.
