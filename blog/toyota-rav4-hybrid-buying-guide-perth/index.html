Buying a used Toyota RAV4 Hybrid in Perth: what to check first

Year-by-year changes, the known issues worth asking about, running costs against the petrol RAV4, and what an inspection should cover.

8 September 20263 min readAutomotive Insight, Shenton Park

0482 097 182

Not long ago, buying a new RAV4 Hybrid meant putting your name down and waiting months. A lot of people didn't want to wait, and the used market filled the gap. The first of these cars are now six or seven years old, some with serious kilometres on them. They're still among the best used SUVs you can buy, but by this age a few things are worth checking.

Key takeaways

  • The RAV4 Hybrid arrived in 2019, and by 2024 every new RAV4 was a hybrid.
  • All-wheel-drive versions have a separate electric motor at the back, and its wiring deserves a close look.
  • Three recalls are worth checking against the VIN.
  • In a side-by-side test, the AWD hybrid used about 2 L/100km less than the petrol version.
  • Where the car was serviced affects how long its battery is covered.

How the RAV4 Hybrid changed over the years

The fifth-generation RAV4 arrived in 2019 as the first RAV4 sold here with a hybrid. It pairs a 2.5-litre petrol engine with electric motors for 160 kW, or 163 kW on the all-wheel-drive version, which adds an electric motor to drive the rear wheels.

In 2022 the XSE grade joined the range, and the hybrid became available on more models. Later cars also gained extra safety features, including rear cross-traffic alert and rear autonomous emergency braking. From late 2022, a bigger 10.5-inch screen with wireless Apple CarPlay arrived. In 2024, Toyota stopped selling the petrol-only RAV4 altogether. An all-new RAV4 followed in 2026, including Toyota's first plug-in hybrid for Australia.

What to check before you buy

Ask for the service history first. RAV4 Hybrids are due every 12 months or 15,000 km. Where the car was serviced matters as much as how often. Toyota covers the hybrid battery for five years, and only extends that to as much as 10 years for cars with yearly health checks and logbook servicing at a Toyota dealer.

A missing warning light tells you very little about the battery itself. A capacity test tells you a lot more. While you're inside, have a look at the battery cooling vent near the back seat. If it's full of dust or dog hair, the battery has probably been running hotter than it should.

On all-wheel-drive cars, get someone to look underneath at the back. In North America, 2019 to 2022 AWD RAV4 Hybrids have had corrosion on the high-voltage connectors at the rear motor, mostly blamed on salted roads. We don't salt our roads in Perth, but a car that's spent time on the beach or near the coast can still suffer. You're looking for crusty orange connectors.

Uneven wear on the tyres usually points to an alignment issue or a run-in with a kerb.

Common issues

The RAV4 Hybrid has an excellent reliability record, but a few known issues are worth checking. Recall REC-001312 covered cracks in the front lower suspension arms on 1,552 RAV4s built in 2019 and 2020. A 2022 software recall fixed stability control that didn't switch back on at start-up on some cars. And in 2025 Toyota recalled nearly 70,000 vehicles, including RAV4s built between July 2022 and April 2025, for an instrument cluster that could go blank.

Some early cars also leaked water around the roof rails, so check the roof lining near the doors for stains. The brakes can suffer from too little use, too. With the electric motor doing so much of the slowing, the discs can rust, particularly on cars kept near the coast.

Running costs and servicing

When CarExpert tested the two side by side, the 2.0-litre petrol RAV4 used 7.6 L/100km and the AWD hybrid used 5.6. Over 15,000 km a year, that's around 300 litres of fuel saved. Capped-price servicing cost the same for both.

Towing is where the hybrid falls short. The 2019 AWD model is rated to 1,500 kg, which rules out a lot of family caravans. Our pre-tow car check explains how to work out your limits.

A proper hybrid service covers the usual oil, filter and fluids, plus battery health, regenerative braking, the high-voltage system and the inverter cooling. That's exactly what our hybrid servicing includes.

Get a pre-purchase inspection

Before you hand over the money, let us test the battery, check the recalls, inspect the rear motor and its wiring, and scan for stored hybrid faults. You'll get a plain-English written report you can use to negotiate, or to walk away.

Common questions

Is a used RAV4 Hybrid a reliable buy?

Generally, yes. Check the battery, the recalls and the service history first.

Is there anything specific to check on an AWD RAV4 Hybrid?

Yes. Look at the rear motor's high-voltage connectors for corrosion, along with the battery, suspension and tyres.

Will servicing it outside Toyota void the warranty?

Not the standard warranty. The extended battery cover beyond five years depends on servicing at Toyota.

How long do RAV4 Hybrid batteries last?

Toyota covers them for five years, and up to 10 under its conditions. How long yours lasts depends on how it's been driven and how hot it's been kept.

Can a RAV4 Hybrid tow a caravan?

Only a small one. The 2019 AWD version is rated to 1,500 kg braked, with a 150 kg tow ball limit.
